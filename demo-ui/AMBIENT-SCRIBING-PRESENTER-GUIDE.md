# Ambient Scribing Runtime Authority Demonstrator — Presenter Guide

**Status:** Reference-model demonstration using synthetic clinical data  
**Audience:** NHS organisations, clinical safety / digital teams, assurance bodies, AI suppliers, technical reviewers  
**Demo location:** `demo-ui/ambient-scribing.html`

> **Core question:** The action was authorised earlier. Is it still authorised now?

## 1. What this demonstration is for

This demonstrator explains a narrow runtime-authority question in an ambient-scribing workflow.

An AI-generated clinical note may have passed earlier workflow checks. Before the represented EPR commit, however, something relevant may have changed. The demonstration asks whether the conditions supporting the proposed action still hold **at the point the consequence is about to be formed**.

FlowSignal represents this as:

**WHO → WHAT → NOW → MATCH**

- **WHO** — who is attempting the action?
- **WHAT** — what action are they attempting?
- **NOW** — does the required authority / standing still hold now?
- **MATCH** — does the action and payload about to execute match what was represented earlier?

The demonstrator then records the evidence used for the represented runtime determination.

## 2. Short introduction to ORCHA's AI Risk Profiler (AiRP)

If the audience needs the ORCHA context, say:

> “ORCHA's AI Risk Profiler is a structured AI-risk assessment approach for digital health. ORCHA describes it as assessing individual AI features against clinical context, computational function, inference technique and model architecture, risk activation, structural and governance mitigations, and residual gaps. It produces an evidence-based risk profile rather than a simple pass/fail result.
>
> This FlowSignal demonstration does not reproduce ORCHA's methodology or questionnaire. It explores a narrower engineering question that may be relevant when considering structural controls: can authority be re-established immediately before an AI-enabled action forms a consequence, and can the evidence for that runtime decision be retained?”

**Presenter boundary:** Do not say that ORCHA has certified, approved, integrated or formally endorsed this demonstrator. The relationship between this runtime control and AiRP remains something to be tested with ORCHA.

## 3. Opening — before clicking anything

Open the demo and leave:

- **WITH FLOWSIGNAL** selected
- **1 · Nothing changed** selected

Say:

> “This is a synthetic ambient-scribing reference model. It is not a live EPR and it isn't testing whether an AI-generated clinical note is medically correct.
>
> We're looking at a different question. An action may have been authorised earlier in a workflow. Is it still authorised at the moment the consequence is about to occur?
>
> Here the represented consequence is committing an AI-generated clinical note into an EPR.”

Point to **WHO / WHAT / NOW / MATCH**.

Say:

> “Immediately before that represented commit, FlowSignal asks four things: who is acting, what they're authorised to do, whether the required standing still holds now, and whether what is about to happen matches what was represented earlier.”

Point to the red **REFERENCE MODEL ENDS HERE** panel.

Say:

> “One important boundary before we start: this visualisation does not demonstrate production standing-source authenticity, independently administered identity or key trust, closure of every route into a real EPR, or physical consequence confirmation. Those are deliberately marked as not demonstrated.”

## 4. Scenario 1 — Nothing changed

Keep **WITH FLOWSIGNAL** and **1 · Nothing changed** selected.

Before clicking, point out:

- T0 Consultation — `VALID · rev 1`
- No relevant change
- NOW — `ACTIVE`
- MATCH — `MATCH`

Say:

> “First, the positive control. Nothing relevant changes. Current standing is ACTIVE and the proposed action still matches.”

Click **Attempt represented EPR commit →**

Expected:

- **ALLOW · COMMITTED**
- EPR Commit box becomes green
- Current standing = ACTIVE
- Payload match = MATCH
- Represented sink = 1 synthetic note

Say:

> “The reference model does not exist to stop actions. When the conditions still hold and the proposed action matches, it allows the represented commit.”

Point to the evidence panel.

Say:

> “Notice that we also retain a contemporaneous decision record: actor, action, target, earlier evidence, current standing, match result, determination and represented outcome. The demo receipt ID and timestamp are generated by this visualisation; they are not production trust anchors.”

## 5. Scenario 2 — Patient dissents: WITHOUT FlowSignal

Select **WITHOUT FLOWSIGNAL**, then **2 · Patient dissents**.

Do **not** click yet.

Say:

> “Now we'll keep the workflow the same but introduce a relevant event between the earlier valid state and the proposed consequence.
>
> At T0 the represented conditions were satisfied. Before the proposed EPR commit, the patient dissents. In this synthetic example that creates new evidence at revision 2.”

Point to:

- T0 = VALID · rev 1
- WHAT CHANGED? = Patient dissents / new evidence · rev 2
- NOW = not re-evaluated
- MATCH = not re-evaluated
- Comparator path = No consequence-time authority check

Say:

> “This WITHOUT path is deliberately a synthetic comparator. It is not a claim that a particular NHS EPR or ambient-scribing product behaves this way. It asks a simple architectural question: what if the earlier state is relied upon and no T1 authority revalidation occurs?”

Click **Attempt represented EPR commit →**

Expected:

- **COMPARATOR · COMMITTED** in amber
- Current standing = NOT RE-EVALUATED
- Payload match = NOT RE-EVALUATED
- Represented sink = 1 synthetic note

Say:

> “The important point isn't that the comparator made the wrong authority decision. It didn't make a consequence-time authority determination at all. The earlier state was relied upon.”

## 6. Scenario 2 — same event WITH FlowSignal

Without changing the scenario, select **WITH FLOWSIGNAL**.

Before clicking, say:

> “Same earlier state. Same note. Same patient dissent. Same actor. Same proposed action. One control difference: current authority is evaluated at the represented consequence boundary.”

Click **Attempt represented EPR commit →**

Expected:

- NOW = PREVENTED
- MATCH = MATCH
- **REFUSE · NOT COMMITTED**
- EPR box red
- Represented sink = empty / unchanged

Say:

> “The note still matches the represented action. The actor hasn't changed. What changed is current standing. The reference model establishes PREVENTED at T1, so the represented commit is refused.”

Point to the evidence panel.

Say:

> “If this decision were challenged later, the useful evidence is not merely ‘FlowSignal blocked it’. The record shows what was known at the decision point: the earlier state, the later change, current standing, payload match, the determination and the represented outcome.”

## 7. Scenario 3 — Approved draft changes

Keep **WITH FLOWSIGNAL** selected. Select **3 · Approved draft changes**.

Before clicking, point out:

- Clinical note = approved draft A
- WHAT CHANGED? = draft A → draft B
- NOW = ACTIVE
- MATCH = NO MATCH

Say:

> “This tests a different problem. Current standing has not disappeared — it remains ACTIVE. But the object presented for execution is no longer the object represented earlier.
>
> Draft A was the earlier represented object. Draft B is now presented at T1.”

Click **Attempt represented EPR commit →**

Expected:

- Current standing = ACTIVE
- Earlier represented object = draft-A
- Proposed object at T1 = draft-B
- Payload match = NO MATCH
- **REFUSE · NOT COMMITTED**
- Represented sink = empty / unchanged

Say:

> “This separates NOW from MATCH. The actor can still have current authority, but that authority should not simply transfer to a different payload.
>
> Being authorised to act is not sufficient if the thing about to happen isn't the thing that was represented earlier.”

Point to the receipt entries `draft-A`, `draft-B`, and `NO MATCH`.

Say:

> “Here the viewer can see the evidence behind the mismatch rather than simply being asked to trust the label NO MATCH.”

## 8. Scenario 4 — Standing dependency positive control

Select **4 · Standing cannot be trusted** and leave the dropdown on **exact typed ACTIVE**.

Say:

> “The previous examples assumed the system could establish current standing. This scenario challenges that dependency itself.
>
> First we use the positive control. The standing reader returns the exact ACTIVE representation required by the reference model.”

Click **Attempt represented EPR commit →**

Expected:

- NOW = exact typed ACTIVE
- Current standing = ACTIVE
- MATCH = MATCH
- **ALLOW · COMMITTED**
- EPR box green
- Represented sink = 1 synthetic note

Say:

> “Again, this isn't a permanent stop button. When current standing can be established as ACTIVE and the proposed action matches, the represented commit proceeds.”

## 9. Scenario 4 — missing standing reader

In **Standing reader result**, choose **no standing reader**.

Click **Attempt represented EPR commit →**

Expected:

- NOW = no standing reader
- Current standing = MISSING
- MATCH = MATCH
- **REFUSE · NOT COMMITTED**
- EPR box red
- Represented sink = empty / unchanged

Say:

> “Nothing here says the patient withdrew anything, that the note is clinically wrong, or that the actor lacks authority. The narrower finding is that current standing cannot be established.
>
> The reference model does not convert missing evidence into permission. It fails closed at the represented protected path.”

## 10. What the evidence panel means

After any completed FlowSignal decision, point to **EVIDENCE USED AT DECISION TIME**.

Say:

> “This is an important part of the proposition. Runtime control without evidence leaves a later assurance problem.
>
> The record is intended to show the basis available to the reference-model decision at T1 rather than reconstructing a justification afterwards.”

The visual record may include:

- demo receipt ID
- decision timestamp
- actor
- action
- target
- earlier evidence
- runtime change
- current standing
- payload match
- determination
- represented sink
- scenario-specific object evidence

Then add:

> “In this visual demonstrator the timestamp and receipt identifier are generated locally. They are useful for demonstrating the evidence model, but they are not being claimed as independently trusted or production-grade evidence.”

## 11. Why T0 can differ from T1

If asked “Why would authority change in the few moments between approval and execution?”, say:

> “Because workflows are not static. New information can arrive after an earlier approval or valid state: a patient position can change, the proposed payload can change, a mandate can be withdrawn, an encounter can close, evidence can become stale, or the system responsible for establishing current standing can become unavailable.
>
> FlowSignal's question is not whether those events always occur. It asks what the execution architecture should do when they do occur.”

For this demo, keep the claim narrow:

- **Patient dissents** is a synthetic workflow event used to illustrate changed standing.
- **Draft A → Draft B** illustrates changed execution content.
- **Missing standing reader** illustrates inability to establish current standing.

Do not present any of these as evidence that a named NHS organisation or supplier has experienced the demonstrated failure.

## 12. AIRP engineering evidence shown at the bottom

If someone asks what AIRP-006 / 008 / 009 mean, say:

> “These are preserved engineering challenges against the reference implementation, not ORCHA AiRP assessment numbers.
>
> AIRP-006 exercised the composition path and positive control.
>
> AIRP-008 exposed a first-run weakness where an earlier capability could still commit after current standing had become PREVENTED. That failure was preserved, the boundary was corrected, and the frozen challenge then passed.
>
> AIRP-009 challenged the standing dependency. Its first run exposed acceptance of a weak ACTIVE representation and an omitted standing reader. Those failures were preserved, remediated, and the challenge rerun to 7 out of 7 pass.
>
> The fourth-order boundary remains deliberately red because external trust and real EPR properties have not been demonstrated by this reference model.”

**Important terminology:** The `AIRP-00x` identifiers in the FlowSignal engineering history are internal FlowSignal challenge identifiers. They are **not** ORCHA's AI Risk Profiler results or identifiers.

## 13. What this demo demonstrates

The safe claim is:

> “Within this synthetic reference model, the represented consequence path re-evaluates current standing and action/payload match immediately before the represented EPR commit, produces ALLOW or REFUSE behaviour for the demonstrated scenarios, and records the evidence used by that determination.”

It also demonstrates that the visual model can distinguish:

- current authority that remains ACTIVE;
- current standing that becomes PREVENTED;
- an ACTIVE actor/action with a mismatched payload;
- inability to establish current standing;
- an exact ACTIVE standing dependency from weaker or missing representations.

## 14. What this demo does NOT demonstrate

Do **not** claim that this visualisation proves:

- a real NHS EPR has been integrated;
- every route to a production EPR is closed;
- physical non-formation of a clinical record has been established;
- the standing source is externally authentic, fresh or rollback-resistant;
- identity/key trust is independently administered;
- the AI-generated note is clinically accurate;
- hallucination, bias, model drift or clinical efficacy have been solved;
- patient dissent automatically has the legal or clinical effect represented in every NHS workflow;
- ORCHA has certified, approved or formally integrated FlowSignal;
- passing the reference-model tests proves production safety.

The red **REFERENCE MODEL ENDS HERE** box exists specifically to keep those boundaries visible.

## 15. Recommended close

Finish on a completed FlowSignal decision with the evidence panel visible.

Say:

> “The point isn't that every AI action should be blocked or escalated. The positive controls show the opposite.
>
> The question is whether an organisation should rely solely on the fact that an action was authorised earlier, or independently establish that the required authority still holds immediately before the consequence is formed.
>
> And if that decision is questioned later, can the organisation show the evidence that was available when the decision was made?
>
> That's the problem FlowSignal is exploring:
>
> **Before AI acts, know it's authorised.**
>
> **Execute with authority. Defend with evidence.**”

## 16. Five-minute version

For a short meeting, run only:

1. **Scenario 1 WITH FlowSignal** — show ACTIVE + MATCH → ALLOW.
2. **Scenario 2 WITHOUT FlowSignal** — patient dissent, no T1 revalidation → comparator committed.
3. **Scenario 2 WITH FlowSignal** — same event → PREVENTED → REFUSE.
4. **Scenario 3 WITH FlowSignal** — ACTIVE but draft-A ≠ draft-B → REFUSE.
5. Point to the contemporaneous evidence record and red reference-model boundary.

Mention Scenario 4 rather than running every dropdown case unless the audience is technical.

## 17. Presenter rules

- Say **represented EPR commit**, not “we stopped a real EPR”.
- Say **reference model**, not “production control”.
- Say **synthetic clinical data / synthetic scenario**, not “NHS incident”.
- Say **evidence used by the reference-model determination**, not “legal proof”.
- Never hide the red external boundary.
- Preserve the distinction between an engineering PASS and an externally demonstrated production property.
- If asked something the demo does not establish, say so plainly and identify what additional integration or evidence would be required.
